\section*{Note S5: ASDS candidate search}

The aligned spatial-distribution similarity (ASDS) was developed after the
original 150-pair ratings were available, and those ratings served as its
development set. Three fixed distribution families were considered: polar
occupancy, \(3\times3\) grid occupancy, and projection profiles. Component
weights were evaluated on a coarse non-negative simplex with step 0.025
against the three-rater mean. The full-grid apparent optimum is
\((w_g,w_p,w_{\mathrm{proj}})=(0.225,0.700,0.075)\) with Spearman
\(\rho=0.557\). Rather than retain the sample-specific secondary weights, the
search-selected polar weight was kept and the remaining 0.30 was split
equally, giving the frozen specification \((0.15,0.70,0.15)\) with
\(\rho=0.556\). Five-fold internal validation groups pairs by character
identity, selects the weights of each fold on the other characters only, and
yields the character-grouped out-of-fold estimate \(\rho=0.540\) reported in
the main text. The individual components reach \(\rho=0.547\) (polar),
\(0.447\) (grid), and \(0.445\) (projection). All of these are development or
internal-validation results on one retrospective cohort; none is an external
or prospective confirmation. The search grid and its selected optimum are recorded in the frozen
development report so that post-rating researcher degrees of freedom remain
visible.
