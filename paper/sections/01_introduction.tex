\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Chinese calligraphy (\emph{shufa}) is both a writing practice and an
important form of Chinese traditional culture. Two handwritten instances of
the same character are often equally legible, yet a calligraphy teacher
would mark different things on each: a
horizontal stroke that stops short, a hook that departs from the wrong point,
a component placed too low, an endpoint that fails to meet its neighbour.
Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}(a) shows such a pair, a regular-script reference
and a running-script candidate of one character: both are recognizable, yet
they differ in stroke proportion, in where strokes meet or cross, and in the
space between components. A learner benefits from knowing which of these
differences matter and where they are.

Not every difference is an error. The character may be written slightly
larger, shifted on the canvas, or tilted by a degree or two without any
change of structure; a missing terminal fragment, an over-wide stroke, or a
misproportioned component is exactly what assessment should retain. A
reference-conditioned assessment therefore has to compensate global placement
while preserving local discrepancy, and to expose that discrepancy as
inspectable evidence rather than as a number alone.

Recognition, style classification, and generation reduce a character image
to an identity, a category, or a plausible glyph
\cite{liu2013casia,zhang2026callinet,xu2026unicalli}; none of them returns
the spatially resolved, direction-specific comparison that this question
requires. Treated as a document-analysis problem, same-character structural
comparison exposes four computer-vision challenges.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[C1] \emph{Overlapping labels under scarce annotation.} At crossings
  and adhesions one pixel belongs to more than one stroke, so a mutually
  exclusive partition cannot represent the label geometry; endpoint evidence
  is sparse; and dense labels are scarce (769 quality-controlled observations
  of 40 character identities, with no trustworthy writer identity).
  \item[C2] \emph{Global nuisance versus local structure.} Direct overlap
  confounds structure with translation, scale, and rotation; flexible
  registration can erase the proportion or placement error that assessment
  should retain; and an overlap score can reward direction channels that are
  empty in both images.
  \item[C3] \emph{Stability is not validity.} A score that is stable under
  allowed transforms may still disagree with how trained readers perceive
  structural similarity.
    \item[C4] \emph{A scalar does not localize.} Feedback must name the
  direction family, the region, and whether ink is missing or extra; fluent
  text cannot repair a wrong attribution.
\end{enumerate}

Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} outlines the formulation studied here: the learner
image and the reference are parsed into overlapping structural channels, the
reference is registered to the learner with a bounded global transform, and
the aligned pair yields a scalar agreement together with directional and
spatial evidence that precedes any text realization. Parsing addresses C1 and registration C2; the score and the evidence
outputs are the objects of C3 and C4, tested by the human study and the
diagnostic benchmark. The paper asks four corresponding research
questions.
\begin{description}
  \item[RQ1 (representation and generalization).] Can convolutional and
  transformer parsers trained on the quality-controlled corpus of
  Sect.~\ref{sec:data} recover overlapping direction masks and sparse
  endpoint masks, and how much does
  performance drop for character identities never seen in training?
  \item[RQ2 (registration and score behaviour).] Relative to no alignment,
  does a constrained similarity registration reduce sensitivity to nuisance
  transforms while preserving penalties for controlled structural changes;
  does a wider search improve that trade-off; and how do the frozen score's
  channel-coverage semantics and same-character discrimination behave on
  real references?
  \item[RQ3 (human validity).] How strongly do three fixed scores of the
  aligned pair, the frozen production score, the coverage-aware score, and
  aligned spatial-distribution similarity (ASDS), associate with blinded
  human ratings of same-character structural similarity, and does the ASDS
  scalar depend on six-channel parsing?
  \item[RQ4 (localized diagnosis).] Which localized diagnostic outputs are
  reliable on interventions with known ground truth, and why do they fail?
\end{description}

The contributions follow the research questions.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item For RQ1, a six-channel representation with five independently
  activated direction-family channels and an endpoint channel, needing no
  per-character prior at inference (unlike Stroke-Seg's stroke instances,
  Sect.~\ref{sec:related}), a semantic quality-control protocol, and a
  matched three-seed comparison of U-Net, DeepLabV3+, and SegFormer-B2 on
  standard and character-disjoint splits. Ranking depends on
  target and protocol: DeepLabV3+ leads in-corpus direction parsing,
  SegFormer-B2 unseen-character direction parsing, and U-Net strict endpoint
  localization.
  \item For RQ2, a constrained similarity registration evaluated with
  controlled nuisance and structural perturbations of 200 real references,
  paired comparisons against no alignment and a wider search, and an
  inactive-channel audit of the score. Constrained registration
  substantially reduces nuisance sensitivity relative to no alignment while
  preserving structural penalties, with residual sensitivity and
  clipping-conditional summaries reported explicitly.
    \item For RQ3, a blinded three-rater association study of the frozen
  production score, the coverage-aware score, and ASDS, reported as
  retrospective development with character-grouped internal validation,
  plus a paired direct-ink control. ASDS associates most strongly with the
  ratings but is a silhouette measure that does not justify the six-channel
  parser, whose value lies in direction-level evidence.
  \item For RQ4, localized structured evidence with a diagnostic failure
  taxonomy. The required finding is usually surfaced, but exact-cell
  localization is unreliable, mainly because multi-cell truth is scored by a
  single-cell metric and direction attribution errs; language generation
  stays downstream of the evidence.
\end{enumerate}

The intended input is a raster exported from a direct digital writing canvas,
not a photograph. The claims concern reference-conditioned structural
analysis in this modality. They do not extend to camera robustness, writer-disjoint generalization,
stroke-order recovery, historical authenticity, or universal aesthetic
grading.
