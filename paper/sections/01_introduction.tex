\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Chinese calligraphy (\emph{shufa}) is both a writing practice and an important
form of Chinese traditional culture. A character is judged not only by its
identity but also by stroke organization, balance, proportion, negative
space, and endpoint relations. Horizontal, vertical, rising-diagonal,
falling-diagonal, and compound or curved strokes frequently adhere or cross
in the final raster. Two samples can therefore be equally recognizable while
differing in precisely the local structure that a learner should revise.

Most computer-vision work on Chinese handwriting emphasizes recognition,
style classification, or generation. These tasks respectively reduce an
image to an identity, a category, or a plausible glyph; none directly answers
the assessment question considered here: given a learner image and an
approved same-character reference, which differences are benign global
placement changes, and which are localized structural discrepancies?
Practice-oriented assessment requires inspectable spatial evidence before
any natural-language feedback is produced.

Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} summarizes our reference-conditioned formulation.
The learner and reference are parsed into overlapping structural channels,
the reference undergoes only bounded global registration, and the aligned
pair yields scalar agreement together with directional and spatial evidence.

The problem exposes four general computer-vision challenges. First, softmax
segmentation cannot represent multiple valid labels at one crossing pixel.
Second, direct overlap confounds structure with translation, scale, and small
rotation. Third, flexible registration can erase the proportion or placement
error that assessment should retain. Fourth, transformation stability does
not establish human validity: a score can reward empty channels or disagree
with perceived spatial organization. These issues also arise in touching
handwriting, layered diagrams, and sparse line art, where overlapping
components, nuisance geometry, and interpretable discrepancy must be handled
jointly under limited dense annotation.

The recovered project corpus contains complete six-channel ground truth for
40 character identities but no trustworthy writer or style provenance.
Accordingly, we report both a quality-controlled standard protocol and a
frozen character-disjoint protocol rather than inferring unseen-structure
generalization from an identity-overlapping split
\cite{baloun2025selfsupervision,oliveira2018dhsegment}. The commissioned
digital acquisition produced complete characters, isolated stroke sources,
and endpoint annotations. Of 894 directories, 840 contained all six channels;
semantic mismatch auditing and exact-duplicate canonicalization yielded 769
QC-clean observations. The external Calli-Tongji Beta subset is used only as
a licence-constrained reference resource, never as parser ground truth
(Fig.~\ref{fig:data-resources}).

We ask four questions: (RQ1) how well do convolutional and transformer
parsers recover overlapping direction and endpoint masks under standard and
unseen-character evaluation; (RQ2) how does constrained registration trade
nuisance reduction against preservation of structural penalties; (RQ3) how
strongly do fixed scores associate with blinded human structural ratings,
including a retrospective spatial score and direct-ink control; and (RQ4)
which localized diagnostic outputs are reliable, and why do they fail?

The principal contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item an independently activated six-channel representation and semantic-QC
  protocol for low-resource overlapping complete-character parsing;
  \item a matched three-architecture, three-seed study on standard and
  character-disjoint splits, with direction, boundary, and endpoint metrics;
  \item discrepancy-preserving registration and score-semantics audits using
  controlled perturbations, paired uncertainty, cross-reference tests, and
  inactive-channel analysis; and
  \item blinded human association, transparent retrospective development of
  aligned spatial-distribution similarity (ASDS), a paired direct-ink
  control, and a diagnostic failure taxonomy separating evidence from
  language realization.
\end{enumerate}

The intended input is a raster exported from a direct digital writing canvas,
not a photograph. The claims concern reference-conditioned structural
analysis in this modality, not camera robustness, writer-disjoint
generalization, stroke-order recovery, historical authenticity, or universal
aesthetic grading.
