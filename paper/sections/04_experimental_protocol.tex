\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\subsection{Legacy segmentation data}

The recovered legacy corpus contains 894 complete-character sample directories
across 43 character IDs. Of these, 840 samples from character IDs \(0\)--\(39\)
contain a complete RGB image, five direction masks, a keypoint mask, and a
stacked six-channel mask. Fifty-four samples from IDs \(40\)--\(42\) lack
direction mappings and are excluded rather than pseudo-labelled. File-level
completeness was followed by semantic QC: 12 source-image/GT mismatches and 59
non-canonical members of exact image-and-mask duplicate groups were excluded
under a frozen rule, leaving 769 unique QC-clean observations. The two
exclusion sets do not overlap. The masks were generated from human
stroke-isolated images and a fixed, character-specific direction mapping. The
recovery and QC provenance are documented in `DATA_RECOVERY_REPORT.md` and
`artifacts/data_qc/DATASET_QC_REPORT_V1.md`.

The original standard assignment contains 600/120/120 samples; after frozen QC
it contains 530/119/120 train/validation/test observations. It is
group-disjoint but not character-disjoint. A second character assignment was
frozen before model evaluation: 28/6/6 characters and originally 588/126/126
samples. Applying the same QC exclusion list yields 539/114/116 observations
with zero character overlap. The source character-assignment SHA-256 is
\texttt{eec9bf5c0910a2e9f6046991f1458519cd903d31deea3e0a4d33c555ff53a09e};
the derived QC-clean split SHA-256 is
\texttt{e9303314d1b70d3f92efcdc5c0807f833148cbe64c2702379f0ac951ed2a1e2b}.

\subsection{Reference library}

The structural experiments use all 200 approved references in the local cache:
100 attributed to the Calli-Tongji Beta Ouyang Xun regular-script category and
100 to the Wang Xizhi running-script category. The cache was created by
SegFormer-B2 v1 and contains six binary masks per reference. It supports seven
same-character cross-style comparisons but no same-style
different-instance comparison.

\subsection{Main segmentation experiments}

We compare U-Net, a ResNet-50 DeepLabV3+ with output stride 16 and ASPP rates
\(6,12,18\), and ImageNet-pretrained SegFormer-B2. Each architecture is trained
with the fixed seeds 20260811, 314159, and 271828. All models share the
six-channel target schema, label-safe augmentation, AdamW optimization,
mixed-precision training, cosine scheduling, and early stopping. Thresholds
are calibrated on the validation split only; the test split is used once for
final evaluation. Metrics are five-direction Macro Dice and Macro IoU,
per-channel precision/recall/Dice, Boundary F1, strict keypoint F1, and
tolerant keypoint F1. All declared seeds are retained.

\subsection{Character-disjoint generalization}

The same three architectures and seeds are rerun on the frozen
character-disjoint split. Test characters do not inform thresholds, learning
rate, augmentation, stopping, or architecture selection. Results are reported
separately from the standard split rather than pooled.

\subsection{Controlled perturbation}

To isolate score and alignment behaviour from segmentation error, deterministic
perturbations are applied directly to the cached real reference masks.
Nuisance families are translation, rotation, up/down scale, and a compound
allowed transform. Structural families are terminal deletion, added fragment,
local fragment shift, direction dilation/erosion, and keypoint shift. Cases
that risk canvas clipping are retained as invalid with a reason and excluded
from valid-score summaries.

For each perturbation we report \(N\), mean, median, sample standard deviation,
5th and 95th percentiles, a 95\% bootstrap confidence interval over references,
invalid fraction, within-reference severity Spearman correlation, and adjacent
non-increasing rate.

\subsection{Alignment ablation}

The same perturbations are evaluated with no alignment, the current constrained
alignment, and a preregistered wider similarity range
(\(s\in[0.6,1.4]\), \(\phi\in[-12^\circ,12^\circ]\)). Comparisons are paired on
reference, perturbation, and severity. Uncertainty is estimated by bootstrap
resampling references. Wilcoxon signed-rank tests and rank-biserial effect sizes
use per-reference mean paired differences.

\subsection{Structure-score audit}

The audit recomputes the production score, examines inactive direction
channels, and compares the current five-channel mean with a coverage-aware
candidate. The candidate remains an audit quantity and does not replace the
production score. Qualitative cases are selected by correction quantiles
(maximum, upper quartile, median, lower quartile, and near-zero positive), not
by visual preference.

\subsection{Cross-reference test}

Pairs are selected independently of their score. The current library supports
seven same-character cross-style pairs and 50 different-character negative
pairs. Self-pairs are prohibited. The absent same-character, same-style,
different-instance condition is reported as unsupported.

\subsection{Diagnostic benchmark}

Perturbation cause supplies deterministic diagnostic ground truth; it is not an
expert aesthetic label. We evaluate required Recall@3, Top-1 cause, direction
channel, missing/extra classification, exact/overlap grid-region localization,
specificity, and center-direction wording. Because broad improvements were not
observed, diagnostic results are treated as secondary and failure analysis is
preferred over further rule tuning.

\subsection{Blinded human structural validation}

We selected 150 natural same-character, different-instance pairs from the 769
QC-clean instances within the recovered 840-file-complete corpus before
collecting ratings. The selection spans all
40 available character identities, with three or four pairs per character.
Writer identity and verified style provenance were unavailable and are not
claimed. The pair set, images, blinded identifiers, and presentation order were
frozen before the first rating. System scores, source-pair identifiers, and
repeat identities were hidden from the raters.

Three consenting human raters independently judged structural similarity on a
five-point ordinal scale. Their self-reported backgrounds were one calligraphy
teacher with one year of learning and teaching experience, one rater with two
years of other relevant experience, and one rater reporting no prior
calligraphy experience. We therefore use the terms ``human raters'' and
``human structural validation'', rather than treating the group as three
qualified calligraphy experts. Each rater evaluated the 150 canonical pairs
and 15 randomly interspersed hidden repeats, for 165 presentations.

System validity is measured by Spearman correlation between score and the
three-rater mean on the 150 canonical presentations. Confidence intervals use
10,000 bootstrap resamples clustered by target character. We report both the
frozen production score and the coverage-aware audit score; the comparison
does not authorize a post-hoc production-formula change. Inter-rater
reliability is estimated by two-way random-effects absolute-agreement
ICC(2,1) and ICC(2,\(k\)) on canonical pairs only. Hidden repeats are excluded
from correlation and ICC and are used solely for within-rater exact agreement,
agreement within one scale point, mean absolute difference, and quadratic
weighted kappa.
