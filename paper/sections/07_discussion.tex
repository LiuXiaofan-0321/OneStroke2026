\section{General Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the four answers imply together}

Read together, the answers describe a measurement design rather than a
single model. First, low-resource dense parsing must be evaluated on the
generalization axis the application needs, since in-corpus fit did not
predict transfer to unseen characters, and only overlapping labels can
express the crossing evidence of Fig.~\ref{fig:segmentation-qualitative}.
Second, registration is part of the measurement, not neutral preprocessing:
the transform family decides which differences remain visible, and the
bounded search was chosen so that global placement, size, and tilt are
compensated while local structural loss stays in view.

Third, the scalar and the diagnostics have different justifications. A direction-label swap that preserves the silhouette would leave ASDS
unchanged while altering every direction-level, endpoint, and localized
output; a system that needs only a scalar silhouette comparison may use
thresholded ink, whereas a diagnostic system requires the parser.

The next steps follow: the frozen ASDS specification should be tested
without modification on new writers, characters, and raters; and diagnostic localization should be reported with a region-overlap or
pixel-overlap metric that credits multi-cell truth.

\subsection{Limitations}

The segmentation corpus contains 40 labelled character identities from one
recovered acquisition source; character-disjoint evaluation is not
writer-disjoint evaluation (Sect.~\ref{sec:data-corpus}), and
collection-specific regularities may survive the semantic quality control.

Controlled perturbations operate on cached reference masks predicted by
the v1 checkpoint, which predates the semantic quality control and the
formal comparison; any systematic parsing error in those masks propagates
into every RQ2 and RQ4 statistic, and an end-to-end failure may combine
parsing, registration, and comparison errors. Scale-up and compound summaries are
conditional on clipping-safe references because of their high invalid
fractions, and the external library supports only seven same-character
cross-style pairs.

The human cohort contains 150 pairs rated by three domain-qualified raters,
two of whom are authors; single-rater reliability is poor and the
three-rater mean moderate; all scores on the cohort are computed from
annotated masks, so parser error in deployment is not included; ASDS was
developed after the ratings were available, so its association is
retrospective and its validation character-grouped rather than external;
the contribution of registration to ASDS was not isolated (the polar term
is centroid- and scale-normalized, so much of its association may survive
without alignment); and the direct-ink control reuses the same cohort.
Agreement at multi-label crossing pixels is not measured separately. The diagnostic benchmark uses engineered interventions rather than teacher
judgments. Finally, the target modality is clean direct digital writing; camera
capture lies outside the claims (Sect.~\ref{sec:introduction}).
