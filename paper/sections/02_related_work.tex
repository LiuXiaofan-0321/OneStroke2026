\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Chinese-character recognition and stroke structure}

Large handwriting databases such as CASIA-HWDB established recognition
benchmarks for online and offline Chinese characters
\cite{liu2013casia}. Recognition labels, however, do not directly supervise the
overlapping direction regions and keypoint evidence needed for localized
practice feedback. Our task is closer to structured foreground parsing than to
closed-set character classification.

Encoder--decoder segmentation networks provide the technical basis for this
parsing. U-Net introduced a widely used multiscale convolutional design
\cite{ronneberger2015unet}; DeepLabV3+ combines atrous spatial pyramids with a
decoder \cite{chen2018deeplabv3plus}; and SegFormer uses hierarchical
transformer features with a lightweight decoder
\cite{xie2021segformer}. We use these as model families rather than claiming a
new architecture. The distinguishing element is the overlapping label schema:
five direction masks and one keypoint mask are predicted with independent
sigmoid activations, not a mutually exclusive softmax.

\subsection{Calligraphy modelling and assessment}

Prior computational calligraphy work has often emphasized recognition, style
classification, or glyph generation. Conditional image-translation systems
such as zi2zi demonstrated cross-style Chinese glyph synthesis
\cite{tian2017zi2zi}. More recent resources, including UniCalli, broaden the
available author, script, glyph, and page-level metadata
\cite{unicalli2026}. These directions are complementary to the present problem:
we do not generate a calligraphic glyph and do not infer universal artistic
quality. Instead, we compare a learner's structure against a specified
reference within a finite course package.

Automated assessment also requires careful claim boundaries. A reference
agreement score may support feedback about position, scale, directional
regions, and keypoints, but it is not equivalent to an expert judgment of
brushwork, historical authenticity, rhythm, or beauty. We therefore validate
only structural similarity with a blinded human rating study and retain
explicit limits on aesthetic interpretation.

\subsection{Reference-based handwriting evaluation}

Template and reference-based systems make differences observable but are
sensitive to nuisance geometry. Direct overlap can penalize an otherwise
similar glyph for harmless placement variation, whereas an overly flexible
warp can remove precisely the local error that feedback should identify. This
trade-off motivates our use of a deliberately constrained similarity transform
and a paired ablation over no alignment, the production range, and a wider
benchmark-only range.

\subsection{Image registration and robust alignment}

Image registration methods range from classical parametric alignment to learned
spatial transformers \cite{zitova2003registration,jaderberg2015stn}. Our
objective is not unrestricted registration. We search only global translation,
isotropic scale, and small rotation on a fixed grid. Nonuniform scaling,
affine shear, and deformable warping are prohibited. The controlled
perturbation benchmark separately measures nuisance suppression and
structural-error preservation.

\subsection{Explainable diagnostic feedback}

Pixel metrics alone do not state what should be changed. Our system therefore
extracts structured evidence---dominant direction channel, missing or extra
foreground, grid region, center offset, scale ratio, and keypoint
agreement---before producing feedback. The benchmark evaluates these normalized
evidence labels without calling a language model. Optional text generation is a
surface-realization layer and is outside the primary claims of this manuscript.
