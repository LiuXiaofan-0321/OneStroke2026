\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The experiments support two conclusions. First, constrained alignment is
essential: without it, nuisance transformations dominate the score and local
structural perturbations are comparatively weak. Second, alignment is not a
complete solution. The present discretized search handles translation well but
remains sensitive to scale and, to a lesser degree, rotation. Widening the
search range did not improve the nuisance result, so further alignment tuning
on the same benchmark would risk evaluation overfitting.

The segmentation comparison adds a distinct generalization result. DeepLabV3+
is strongest on the QC-clean standard split, but SegFormer-B2 has the highest
character-disjoint direction overlap and the smallest Macro-Dice decrease
between the two protocols. U-Net instead retains the highest strict keypoint
F1. This architecture-by-target interaction argues against selecting a model
from one aggregate metric alone. It also shows why the preliminary
non-character-disjoint SegFormer release was insufficient evidence for
unseen-character generalization.

The inactive-channel audit exposes a semantic decision hidden by a simple
macro-average. When a direction is absent from both masks, a Dice score of one
is mathematically conventional but may grant credit unrelated to the active
structure. The mean effect is small, yet a limited number of cases receive a
large correction. The blinded human study strengthens this concern:
coverage-aware scoring correlates more strongly with the human mean than the
production score, with a positive character-cluster bootstrap interval for the
correlation difference. Nevertheless, because this comparison was motivated by
the audit and evaluated on the same human study, it is evidence for a future
preregistered score revision rather than permission to replace the frozen
production formula post hoc.

The diagnostic analysis also separates model limitations from metric
limitations. Exact single-cell localization is ill matched to perturbations
that cross grid boundaries or occupy multiple cells. A future benchmark should
represent affected regions as a set or pixel support and score set overlap.
This observation does not justify retroactively altering the current ground
truth or tuning the rules to the present cases.

The study has important limitations. The legacy segmentation corpus contains
only 40 fully labelled character identities and incomplete writer/source
metadata. Character-disjoint evaluation therefore measures unseen character
identity, not unseen writer identity. The reference library contains two style
categories, only seven cross-style same-character pairs, and no same-style
different-instance pairs. All completed robustness experiments operate on
cached masks and therefore isolate scoring from segmentation errors.
End-to-end robustness still requires the pending smartphone and unseen-writer
study. The human validation includes only three raters with heterogeneous
self-reported backgrounds. Its moderate single-rater reliability and higher
average-rating reliability support a structural-similarity claim, but neither
the raters nor the task establish expert aesthetic calibration.

\subsection{Deployment implications}

The frozen SegFormer-B2 parser benefits from a GPU for responsive inference,
whereas alignment, scoring, and deterministic feedback can run on CPU after
masks are available. Reference masks are precomputed. A low-cost deployment can
therefore use one on-demand model service and an external text API, while future
work may distil the parser for ONNX/INT8 edge inference. Deployment
optimization is outside the current scientific claims.
