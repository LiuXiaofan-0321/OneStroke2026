\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We formulated the assessment of a handwritten Chinese character against a
same-character reference as overlapping document parsing,
discrepancy-preserving registration, and human-audited comparison, and
answered four research questions under limited labelled data. For RQ1 (representation and generalization), six independently activated
channels let U-Net, DeepLabV3+, and SegFormer-B2 recover overlapping
direction and endpoint masks from a 769-sample quality-controlled corpus; DeepLabV3+ leads standard-split
direction parsing, SegFormer-B2 leads character-disjoint direction parsing, and U-Net leads strict endpoint localization. For RQ2 (registration and score behaviour), constrained similarity
registration reduces
nuisance sensitivity by 30.118 points relative to no alignment while
preserving 11.189 additional points of structural penalty, and a wider search
does not improve the trade-off; the claim is relative and conditional on
clipping-safe references. For RQ3 (human validity), blinded structural ratings by three
domain-qualified raters, two of them authors, associate modestly with the frozen production score, more strongly with the
coverage-aware score, and most strongly with ASDS, whose \(\rho=0.556\) is a
retrospective development result and whose \(\rho=0.540\) is a
character-grouped internal-validation estimate with an unresolved advantage
over the coverage-aware score; direct-ink and annotated-mask-union ASDS are equivalent, so the parser's
justification is directional, overlap, endpoint, and localized evidence
rather than the scalar. For RQ4 (localized diagnosis), the evidence layer usually surfaces the
required finding but does not reliably localize it to one cell; in 53.9\% of exact-cell failures
the true change spans several cells, which the exact-one-cell metric cannot
credit.

The contribution is an auditable framework for reference-conditioned
structural assessment in the direct-digital modality: semantic quality
control, character-disjoint testing, bounded registration, score-semantics
audits, blinded human association with honest labelling of retrospective
development, and structured evidence kept upstream of language. It is not a universal aesthetic score.
