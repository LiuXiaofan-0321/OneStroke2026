\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Main segmentation accuracy}

\input{tables/task1_main_benchmark}

DeepLabV3+ obtains the highest standard-split direction Macro Dice
\(0.9630\pm0.0006\), Macro IoU \(0.9287\pm0.0012\), and Boundary F1
\(0.8443\pm0.0028\). SegFormer-B2 is close on direction overlap but has greater
between-seed variation. U-Net has lower direction and boundary performance,
while achieving the highest strict keypoint F1
\(0.7562\pm0.0038\). Thus no single architecture dominates both dense
direction segmentation and sparse keypoint localization.

The frozen single-release SegFormer-B2 result is retained only as preliminary
engineering evidence: direction Macro Dice \(0.9610\), Macro IoU \(0.9251\),
Boundary F1 \(0.8348\), and strict keypoint F1 \(0.7159\). It must not substitute
for the planned multi-seed Task~1 comparison. No exact duplicate group crosses
the original standard train/validation/test split, so this preliminary score is
not attributable to exact train-to-test duplicate leakage. However, its
training data contained 12 source-image/GT mismatches and duplicate-weighted
observations; the formal comparison therefore uses the frozen 769-sample
QC-clean population.

\subsection{Controlled perturbation behaviour}

\input{tables/controlled_perturbation}

Table~\ref{tab:controlled} shows that nuisance performance is not uniform.
Translation is exactly recovered in all 800 valid observations. Rotation has a
mean absolute penalty of \(5.395\), while scale-up and scale-down produce larger
penalties of \(13.653\) and \(16.356\), respectively. Scale-up also has a
49.0\% invalid fraction because larger transformed foregrounds risk clipping.
The compound transform has a 54.0\% invalid fraction. These cases are not
silently counted as robustness failures.

Structural perturbations generally decrease the score monotonically. Direction
dilation and erosion have the largest average penalties, followed by terminal
deletion and added fragments. Local fragment displacement and keypoint shifts
are more weakly penalized. The result supports sensitivity to local structure
but also identifies scale invariance as the main nuisance weakness.

\subsection{Paired alignment ablation}

\input{tables/alignment_ablation}

Relative to no alignment, the constrained method reduces the nuisance penalty
by \(30.118\) points (reference-bootstrap 95\% CI \(29.506\)--\(30.727\)) and
increases the preserved structural penalty by \(11.189\) points
(95\% CI \(10.094\)--\(12.322\)). Both per-reference signed-rank comparisons
are strongly nonzero with rank-biserial effect size \(1.0\).

The wider alignment is not superior. The current range has a \(2.896\)-point
lower nuisance penalty (95\% CI \(2.757\)--\(3.034\)), while the wider range
preserves \(0.284\) points more structural penalty on average. Thus the
preregistered expectation that a wider transform would better suppress
nuisance while clearly masking structural errors is not supported. The current
range provides the better nuisance result, whereas the small structural
difference favours the wider range.

The perturbation-level paired breakdown is reported in
Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:alignment-breakdown}; it is derived from the same
reference/perturbation/severity tuples and is not an unpaired post-hoc
comparison.

\subsection{Structure-score audit}

\input{tables/structure_score_audit}

Forty-one of 200 references contain at least one inactive direction channel:
36 contain one inactive channel and five contain two. No reference contains
three or more inactive channels. The distribution is similar across the two
styles, although two-channel inactivity occurs more often in the Wang Xizhi
subset (4/100) than the Ouyang Xun subset (1/100).

Empty-channel agreement affects 1,252 valid perturbation observations. Replacing
the all-five mean with an active-channel mean produces an average downward
correction of \(0.289\) points and a maximum of \(15.402\) points. The largest
cases combine two inactive direction channels with severe perturbation of an
active channel: the fixed all-five macro receives perfect Dice credit from the
empty channels while the active-channel evidence degrades. The audit motivates
expert review of score semantics but does not authorize a production formula
change.

\subsection{Cross-reference semantics}

\input{tables/cross_reference}

The seven same-character cross-style pairs have a mean score of \(20.239\)
(95\% bootstrap CI \(15.639\)--\(25.069\)), compared with \(9.741\)
(\(8.584\)--\(10.970\)) for 50 different-character negatives. Cliff's delta is
\(0.84\). This supports a reference-conditioned same-character signal, but the
small cross-style sample and absence of same-style different-instance pairs
preclude a strong generalization claim.

\subsection{Diagnostic feedback and failure analysis}

\input{tables/feedback_failure_taxonomy}

Required Recall@3 remains \(0.701\) before and after the audited wording change,
while exact region localization is only \(0.109\). Failure taxonomy shows that
53.9\% of exact-region failures arise when the perturbation truth covers
multiple grid cells although the metric requires an exact single-cell result.
Wrong direction channels account for 24.6\%, and absence of a local finding
from the top three accounts for 20.1\%. Wrong missing/extra type and
alignment-residual region shifts are comparatively rare. These results support
keeping the feedback benchmark secondary and revising future evaluation design
rather than tuning rules on the current benchmark.

\subsection{Blinded human structural validation}

\input{tables/human_validation}
\input{tables/rater_reliability}

Across 150 canonical pairs, the production score has a positive but modest
association with the mean blinded human rating
(\(\rho=0.297\), character-cluster bootstrap 95\% CI
\(0.148\)--\(0.441\), \(p=0.00023\)). The coverage-aware audit score is more
strongly associated with the same ratings
(\(\rho=0.429\), 95\% CI \(0.313\)--\(0.535\),
\(p<10^{-7}\)). The paired bootstrap difference in correlation is \(0.132\)
(95\% CI \(0.062\)--\(0.197\), Monte Carlo \(p<0.001\)). This result supports
the semantic concern raised by inactive direction channels, but the
coverage-aware score remains an audit quantity rather than a retroactive
replacement for the frozen production formula.

Canonical inter-rater reliability is \(0.448\) for ICC(2,1) and \(0.709\) for
ICC(2,\(k\)). Across the 45 rater-level hidden-repeat comparisons, exact
agreement is 48.9\%, agreement within one scale point is 93.3\%, mean absolute
difference is 0.578, and pooled quadratic weighted kappa is 0.634. Individual
rater correlations with the production score range from \(0.174\) to \(0.350\)
and are positive for all three raters. These results provide convergent
evidence for reference-conditioned structural similarity while also showing
that the ordinal task and rater pool contain meaningful subjectivity.

\subsection{Character-disjoint generalization}

\input{tables/character_disjoint}

All architectures degrade when test character identities are excluded from
training. SegFormer-B2 gives the strongest character-disjoint direction result,
with Macro Dice \(0.8866\pm0.0008\), Macro IoU
\(0.7977\pm0.0013\), and Boundary F1 \(0.6851\pm0.0046\).
Its Macro Dice decreases by 7.150 percentage points from the standard split,
compared with 9.664 points for DeepLabV3+ and 9.360 points for U-Net.
DeepLabV3+ remains second in direction overlap, whereas U-Net retains the
highest strict keypoint F1. These results identify SegFormer-B2 as the strongest
of the evaluated architectures for unseen-character direction-structure
generalization, not as a universal winner across every output channel.

\subsection{Application and course-scoring interpretation}

The deployed application uses direct digital writing, matching the broad input
modality of the held-out legacy images; a smartphone benchmark is therefore
not an application-relevant missing experiment. The course-overlap audit finds
that none of the 40 recovered legacy characters occurs in either approved
100-character course pack, leaving zero valid same-character legacy-to-course
test cases. We consequently do not manufacture cross-character scores.

Course-scoring validity is instead supported by two complementary completed
experiments. The 150-pair blinded human study evaluates natural
same-character, different-instance structural agreement, while the
course-reference test evaluates all seven same-character cross-style pairs
available in the two approved reference libraries. The former establishes a
human structural signal and the latter establishes reference sensitivity, but
neither supports an expert-calibrated aesthetic-grade claim.
