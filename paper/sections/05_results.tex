\subsection{RQ1: segmentation and unseen-character generalization}

\input{tables/task1_main_benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:task1-main} shows that, on the QC-clean standard split,
DeepLabV3+ obtains the highest direction Macro
Dice (\(0.9630\pm0.0006\)), Macro IoU
(\(0.9287\pm0.0012\)), and Boundary F1
(\(0.8443\pm0.0028\)). SegFormer-B2 is close in direction overlap but shows
greater between-seed variation. U-Net is weaker on direction and boundary
metrics yet gives the best strict endpoint F1
(\(0.7562\pm0.0038\)). Thus, dense direction segmentation and sparse endpoint
localization favour different architectures.

\input{tables/character_disjoint}

All models degrade when character identities are held out
(Table~\ref{tab:character-disjoint}). SegFormer-B2 gives
the strongest character-disjoint direction result: Macro Dice
\(0.8866\pm0.0008\), Macro IoU \(0.7977\pm0.0013\), and Boundary F1
\(0.6851\pm0.0046\). Its Macro-Dice decrease from the standard split is 7.150
percentage points, versus 9.664 for DeepLabV3+ and 9.360 for U-Net.
DeepLabV3+ remains second for direction overlap, while U-Net retains the
highest strict endpoint F1. SegFormer-B2 is therefore the strongest evaluated
model for unseen-character direction parsing, not a universal winner across
all six outputs.

\subsection{RQ2: registration, score behaviour, and semantic audits}

\input{tables/controlled_perturbation}

Table~\ref{tab:controlled} shows heterogeneous residual nuisance sensitivity.
Translation is recovered exactly in 800 valid cases; mean penalties are
5.395 for rotation, 13.653 for scale-up, and 16.356 for scale-down.
Scale-up and compound transforms have 49.0\% and 54.0\% invalid fractions
because of clipping risk. Their summaries are conditional on clipping-safe
references and do not establish full-library invariance.

Direction dilation and erosion produce the largest structural penalties,
followed by terminal deletion and added fragments; local-fragment and
endpoint shifts are weaker. Full distributional statistics appear in
Supplementary Table S1.

\input{tables/alignment_ablation}

The paired comparison in Table~\ref{tab:alignment} shows that, relative to no
alignment, the constrained method reduces nuisance penalties by
30.118 points (reference-bootstrap 95\% CI 29.506--30.727) and preserves an
additional 11.189 points of structural penalty
(10.094--12.322). Both per-reference signed-rank comparisons are nonzero with
rank-biserial effect size 1.0.

The wider range has a 2.896-point higher nuisance penalty
(2.757--3.034), while preserving only 0.284 additional points of structural
penalty. It therefore does not improve the measured trade-off; paired
family-level results appear in Supplementary Table S2.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Score semantics and cross-reference tests.}

Forty-one of 200 references contain inactive direction channels (36 with one,
five with two). Empty-channel agreement affects 1,252 valid perturbation
rows; active-channel averaging changes the score by \(-0.289\) points on
average and at most \(-15.402\). Representative correction cases and the full
audit are supplied in the supplement.

\input{tables/cross_reference}

The seven same-character cross-style pairs score 20.239 on average
(95\% CI 15.639--25.069), versus 9.741 (8.584--10.970) for 50
different-character negatives (Cliff's \(\delta=0.84\))
\cite{cliff1993dominance}. The small positive set and missing
same-style/different-instance condition preclude a broad generalization claim.

\subsection{RQ3: blinded human validity and ASDS development}

\input{tables/human_validation}
\input{tables/rater_reliability}

Across 150 canonical pairs, \(S_{\mathrm{prod}}\) correlates modestly with
the three-rater mean (\(\rho=0.297\), character-cluster 95\% CI
0.148--0.441), while the coverage-aware audit reaches 0.429
(0.313--0.535); their paired difference is 0.132 (0.062--0.197)
(Table~\ref{tab:human-validation}). ICC(2,1) is 0.448 and ICC(2,\(k\))
is 0.709. Hidden repeats yield 48.9\% exact agreement, 93.3\% agreement
within one point, MAD 0.578, and pooled quadratic weighted kappa 0.634
(Table~\ref{tab:rater-reliability}), supporting aggregation while confirming
meaningful subjectivity.

\input{tables/spatial_score_development}

ASDS reaches \(\rho=0.556\) (0.454--0.651), and character-grouped
out-of-fold weight selection retains 0.540 (0.432--0.639). Paired intervals
for ASDS minus production and coverage-aware scoring are 0.110--0.410 and
0.017--0.239; the grouped out-of-fold difference from coverage-aware scoring
is less certain (\(-0.003\)--0.224). The polar component is strongest
(\(\rho=0.547\)); grid and projection reach 0.447 and 0.445
(Table~\ref{tab:spatial-score-development}). Because the same ratings informed
feature design, these are retrospective development and internal-validation
results, not external confirmation.

\input{tables/direct_ink_asds}

Parsed-union and direct-ink ASDS reach \(\rho=0.55623\) and 0.55583
(Table~\ref{tab:direct-ink-asds}); \(\Delta\rho=0.00040\), 95\% CI
\(-0.01011\)--\(0.01030\), and their mutual rank association is 0.9973.
Thus ASDS is a silhouette score on clean digital input; parsing remains
necessary for the separately evaluated direction, overlap, endpoint, and
localized evidence.

\subsection{RQ4: diagnostic feedback}

\input{tables/feedback_failure_taxonomy}

Required Recall@3 is 0.701, whereas exact grid localization is 0.109.
Among exact-region failures, 53.9\% reflect multi-cell truth under a
single-cell metric, 24.6\% use the wrong direction, and 20.1\% omit a local
finding from the top three (Table~\ref{tab:feedback-failures}). The main
limitations are therefore localization representation and direction
attribution, not wording.
