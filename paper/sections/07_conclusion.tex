\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented an auditable pipeline for low-resource Chinese calligraphy
structural analysis based on overlapping direction and keypoint masks,
constrained reference alignment, fixed structure agreement, and localized
diagnostic evidence. Real-reference perturbation experiments show that the
alignment fully absorbs translation, substantially improves over no alignment,
and preserves structural penalties, while scale remains a clear weakness.
Score and diagnostic audits expose inactive-channel bias and limitations of
single-cell feedback localization. Blinded human ratings on 150 natural
same-character pairs show a positive association with the production score and
a stronger association with the coverage-aware audit, while also documenting
non-negligible rater subjectivity. These findings support the pipeline as a
reference-conditioned structural-analysis tool, not as a calibrated
calligraphy grader. Submission-level claims still await the frozen Task~1,
character-disjoint, and smartphone experiments.
